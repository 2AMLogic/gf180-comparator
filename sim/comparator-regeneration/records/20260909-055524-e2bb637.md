- **Note**: MISMATCH-FREE BY CONSTRUCTION. sw_stat_mismatch is left at the PDK default (0), so every device is nominal. Regeneration time and offset are separable effects and are budgeted separately; combining them here would make neither attributable.
- **Note**: THE DELAY IS MEASURED ON THE SECOND STROBE, where the output must actively flip a latch holding the opposite answer. That is why every instance is driven by a PWL that changes sign between the two strobes, and why dout_*_first / dout_*_end are checked as a pair: together they prove one instance decided both ways in one run, so no delay in this record depends on which state the output happened to start in.
- **Note**: TIMING THRESHOLDS ARE SUPPLY-NORMALIZED (each output divided by its own supply, crossed at 0.5). A fixed absolute threshold would turn the +/-10 % supply axis into a measurement artefact rather than a measurement.
- **Note**: METASTABILITY IS A FIRST-CLASS ROW HERE, not a note. tau and resolve_decades are recorded at every PVT point precisely so that any metastability statement this repo makes is computed from a measured time constant at the worst corner rather than asserted from a typical-corner delay.
- **Note**: e_dec_fj CARRIES NO CHECK, DELIBERATELY. The placeholder DUT's decision stage is behavioural and draws no supply current at all, so the switching energy measured here is the FRONT END's alone and what remains of the charge integral is dominated by numerical residue (it moves over three orders of magnitude across the grid, which is a property of the integral, not of the circuit). Rather than invent a floor that would pass for the wrong reason, the number is recorded and left unchecked; a check belongs here the moment a transistor-level decision stage is bound in sim/dut.json.
- **Note**: THE 20 ns INPUT-FLIP INSTANT IS NOT ARBITRARY. The front end's output pole is roughly (load resistance x load capacitance); flipping the input 20 ns before the second strobe leaves it more than ten time constants to settle, so the decision is taken on a settled overdrive rather than on a transient. A bench that strobed too soon would report the settling time, not the regeneration time.
- **Note**: SOLVER TOLERANCES. reltol = 1e-4 and vntol = 1e-9 are tightened from the ngspice defaults so that a sub-nanosecond delay difference between two overdrives is resolved rather than smeared. abstol is 1e-13 and NOT the 1e-15 the kickback deck uses: at 1e-15 six of the 45 points of this grid fail to take their first transient step at all (`Timestep too small ... trouble with node eanc#branch`, at t < 1e-13 s), because the three PWL-driven controlled sources here present branch currents far below that floor at t = 0. A re-run that changes any of these three is not bit-comparable with this record.
